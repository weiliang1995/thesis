#### 4.1.1 Quasi-Experimental Design

This study will apply a quasi-experimental design for 12 weeks to evaluate the effectiveness of Trade-Harness during its application in an industry practice. In this experiment, Trade-Harness was compared to a conventional prompt-based AI-assisted workflow. The quasi-experimental approach is chosen because in a real development process, each task is real and cannot be repeated. Furthermore, task allocation is determined by technical leader based on the assessment of the team's manpower available, making it impractically to achieve the completely random allocation and repeated execution of the same task for cross-validation as in an experiment setting. Under this setup, the study will involve two developers, each using both workflows to complete the tasks. This design aims to reduce developer bias. Besides, tasks were categorized by difficulty to avoid inconsistencies in the results due to differences in difficulty.

This study's experimental setup is based on a front-end system of a cryptocurrency trading platform currently under maintenance. This system is relatively large, with approximately 722,000 lines of code and nearly 70 component libraries maintained by 13 front-end developers. Since business logic, components and dependencies are spread across different repositories and modules, development tasks require developers to work across multiple codebase and validate the changes. Therefore, this system is suitable for observing the differences between Trade-Harness and conventional prompt-based workflows in an industry front-end development.
