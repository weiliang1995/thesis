### 4.6.3 Supplementary E2E Test-Script Correction Loops

Correction loops cover agent-identified and corrected **generated test-script** errors followed by E2E reruns. The initial execution does not count as a loop, and changes to business code are excluded from this definition. Both workflow conditions received the common three-loop instruction.

**Table 4.14. Final E2E test-script correction loops by workflow**

| Measure | Baseline | Trade-Harness |
|---|---:|---:|
| Usable issue records (n) | 158 | 130 |
| Mean loops / issue | 0.95 | 0.71 |
| Median loops / issue | 1.00 | 0.50 |
| Issues with 0 / 1 / 2 / 3 loops | 66 / 51 / 30 / 7 | 65 / 43 / 17 / 5 |
| Issues above 3 loops | 4 | 0 |
| Mean final E2E pass rate among issues with both loop/E2E data (%) | 90.79 | 92.83 |

The mean number of recorded correction loops was 0.95 for the baseline and 0.71 for Trade-Harness. Four baseline issues exceeded the instructed three-loop limit (**DBU-2345, DBU-2390, DBU-2396 and SBU-997**); no Trade-Harness issue exceeded three loops in the current task records. One issue in each workflow lacks a usable correction-loop record.

By complexity, the mean correction-loop counts are Baseline: 0.76 (n=123); Trade-Harness: 0.63 (n=104) for simple tasks; Baseline: 1.52 (n=31); Trade-Harness: 0.76 (n=21) for medium tasks; and Baseline: 2.25 (n=4); Trade-Harness: 2.00 (n=5) for complex tasks. Variation in loop frequency is a supplementary process observation, not direct evidence that the generated business code is better or that a particular context or memory mechanism caused the differences.

### 4.6.4 Cross-Metric Patterns and Trade-offs

In the overall sample, Trade-Harness shows a modestly lower average lead time, a higher mean task-level E2E pass rate and a lower mean defect density, accompanied by a higher average recorded LLM cost and a lower mean changed-LOC-per-million-tokens ratio. The patterns are not uniform across task complexity. In medium tasks, for example, the mean E2E pass rate is higher but median lead time and mean cost are also higher; in complex tasks, the direction of the defect-density comparison differs from the overall comparison.

The results therefore do not support a simple claim that one workflow is uniformly better. The nonrandom allocation, differences in task and developer composition, uneven sample sizes and repeated tasks within developers are important constraints. No statistical significance test is included at this drafting stage. Further interpretation, including whether specific engineering trade-offs are practically meaningful, is reserved for the discussion chapter.
