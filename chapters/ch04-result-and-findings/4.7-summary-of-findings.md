## 4.7 Summary of Findings

> **Provisional summary:** These findings use all 290 issue-level records currently available in the Google Sheet (159 baseline and 131 Trade-Harness). The final study design and dataset eligibility rules are not yet confirmed.

This chapter compared the two AI-assisted development workflows through task-level delivery efficiency, final E2E testing and confirmed defects, and recorded model-token resource efficiency and cost. The results were summarized across the full dataset and then examined by pre-task complexity and developer, with missing measurements and undefined ratios excluded only from the outcomes they affected.

**Table 4.15. Summary of provisional descriptive findings**

| Research question | Principal measures | Overall observed comparison | Stratified pattern and limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| RQ1 – Delivery efficiency | Elapsed development lead time (days) | Mean 2.01 vs 1.91; median 1.01 vs 0.93 (baseline vs Trade-Harness) | Trade-Harness has lower median lead time in simple and complex tasks, but higher in medium tasks; only 4 vs 5 complex issues |
| RQ2 – Software quality | Final task-level E2E pass rate; confirmed defects per 1,000 changed production LOC | Mean E2E 90.79% vs 92.83%; mean defect density 0.93 vs 0.72 | E2E means favour Trade-Harness in each complexity subgroup; complex-task mean defect density goes in the opposite direction (n=3 vs 5 valid) |
| RQ3 – Resource efficiency and cost | Changed LOC per million tokens; gateway LLM cost / issue (USD) | Mean changed LOC/Mtok 20.53 vs 14.46; mean cost $97.05 vs $116.81 | Mean LLM cost is higher under Trade-Harness for simple and medium tasks and almost equal for complex tasks; change-volume/token ratios should not be read as functional productivity |
| Supplementary process | E2E test-script correction loops | Mean 0.95 vs 0.71; 4 vs 0 recorded breaches of the three-loop instruction | Descriptive process observation only; two missing loop records |

The overall averages suggest a trade-off: the current Trade-Harness group has somewhat shorter recorded development lead time and more favourable mean quality measurements, alongside greater mean LLM spending per issue. Those averages do not describe every complexity category. In particular, the small number of complex tasks and the concentration of simpler work in both workflow groups limit what can be concluded about whether Trade-Harness is more useful for demanding tasks.

The study was embedded in nonrandomized production work, and each developer completed multiple tasks. It would therefore be inappropriate to infer that Trade-Harness alone caused the observed differences or that an individual orchestration mechanism explains them. The meaning and practical significance of these patterns, alongside threats to validity, belong in the subsequent discussion chapter.
