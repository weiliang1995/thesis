## 4.3 Delivery Efficiency Results (RQ1)

> **Draft status — provisional dataset.** This analysis includes all 290 tasks and ten developers in the current Google Sheet. The Methodology branch still describes a two-developer design; the final participant and task inclusion rules require confirmation before submission. The figures below are descriptive observations, not causal estimates.

Development lead time is the elapsed time between the Jira issue's first recorded *In Progress* transition and its final recorded *Development Complete* transition, measured in **calendar days**, including non-working time. This is not active coding time or time spent by the agent alone. For reopened issues, the interval spans the repeated development cycles as recorded in the provisional task table (see Section 4.2.3).

### 4.3.1 Overall Lead Time Comparison

**Table 4.4. Development lead time by workflow (elapsed days)**

| Statistic | Baseline | Trade-Harness |
|---|---:|---:|
| Usable issues (n) | 159 | 131 |
| Mean | 2.01 | 1.91 |
| Median | 1.01 | 0.93 |
| Sample standard deviation | 2.59 | 2.50 |
| Interquartile range (IQR) | 2.24 | 2.57 |
| Minimum–maximum | 0.07–19.97 | 0.08–13.93 |

The baseline issues have a mean lead time of **2.01 days**, compared with **1.91 days** for Trade-Harness. The observed difference is **-0.10 days** (-4.8% relative to the baseline). The median is **1.01 days** in the baseline condition and **0.93 days** in Trade-Harness, a difference of **-0.08 days** (-7.9%). Under the current dataset, both the mean and median are slightly lower for Trade-Harness.

Lead-time distributions are right-skewed, as both means exceed the corresponding medians. The baseline includes an observed maximum of 19.97 days, compared with 13.93 days under Trade-Harness. The IQR is 2.24 days for the baseline and 2.57 days for Trade-Harness, so the latter does not show a uniformly narrower central distribution. These results describe the tasks observed under each condition and should not be treated as an isolated causal workflow effect.

### 4.3.2 Lead Time Across Task Complexity

**Table 4.5. Lead time by workflow and pre-task complexity (elapsed days)**

| Complexity | Baseline n | Baseline mean | Baseline median | Trade-Harness n | Trade-Harness mean | Trade-Harness median | Median difference (Trade-Harness − Baseline) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Simple | 124 | 1.17 | 0.91 | 104 | 1.01 | 0.85 | -0.06 |
| Medium | 31 | 3.93 | 3.88 | 22 | 4.03 | 4.17 | 0.29 |
| Complex | 4 | 13.03 | 13.10 | 5 | 11.34 | 11.22 | -1.88 |

For **simple tasks**, Trade-Harness has a median lead time of 0.85 days, compared with 0.91 days under the baseline (-0.06 days). In the **medium** category, Trade-Harness has a higher median (4.17 versus 3.88 days). The **complex** category shows a lower Trade-Harness median (11.22 versus 13.10 days), but this comparison is based on only **4 baseline and 5 Trade-Harness tasks**. It is therefore especially vulnerable to the influence of individual tasks and differences in developer allocation.

The differences do not follow one direction across all complexity levels. Although Trade-Harness shows a shorter median for simple and complex tasks, the medium category shows the opposite direction. These are descriptive subgroup comparisons and do not demonstrate that the workflow's effect changes systematically with complexity.

### 4.3.3 Lead Time by Developer

**Table 4.6. Median development lead time by developer and workflow (elapsed days)**

| Developer | Baseline n | Baseline median | Trade-Harness n | Trade-Harness median |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| D01 | 5 | 1.33 | 7 | 4.27 |
| D02 | 23 | 1.05 | 20 | 0.93 |
| D03 | 18 | 1.09 | 15 | 0.90 |
| D04 | 8 | 3.05 | 9 | 0.96 |
| D05 | 14 | 1.08 | 9 | 0.81 |
| D06 | 24 | 0.97 | 16 | 0.88 |
| D07 | 13 | 0.99 | 13 | 0.94 |
| D08 | 12 | 0.94 | 10 | 0.98 |
| D09 | 21 | 1.00 | 18 | 0.96 |
| D10 | 21 | 0.87 | 14 | 0.84 |

Trade-Harness has a lower median lead time for **8 of the 10 developers**, while the remaining 2 show a higher median. This pattern is not uniform in magnitude, and the developers completed different numbers and types of tasks under the two workflows. In particular, complex tasks are concentrated among a subset of developers and are not evenly represented across workflow conditions. Differences at the developer level should therefore be interpreted together with the complexity distribution in Table 4.2 rather than as independent replications of an experiment.

For the current sample, the observed overall difference in lead time is modest. The analysis does not establish that Trade-Harness alone accounts for the shorter lead time in either the overall group or specific developer/complexity subgroups.

**Analysis notes.** Statistics in this draft are computed using the non-missing task-level `lead_time_days` values supplied in the workbook, which agree with timestamp differences to the nearest 0.01 day. Standard deviation uses the sample formula (denominator `n − 1`); Q1 and Q3 use linear-interpolated 25th and 75th percentiles, with IQR = Q3 − Q1. The reported results are rounded to two decimal places. No significance test or adjustment for developer/task mix is applied at this stage.
