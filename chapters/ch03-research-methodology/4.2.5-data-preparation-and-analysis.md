#### 4.2.5 Data Preparation and Analysis

The analysis uses the Jira issue as the unit of observation. Each eligible feature-development task contributes one record under the workflow condition in which it was completed; the same task is not repeated under both conditions. The preparation and comparison procedures are applied consistently to the baseline and Trade-Harness groups.

**Data Preparation**

Records from the existing AI-development dashboard, its underlying Jira, GitLab and LLM gateway sources, and the branch-named E2E reports are matched to the corresponding Jira issues. The dataset retains the assigned workflow, developer, pre-task complexity level, task status timestamps, changed production-code lines, confirmed linked defects, final E2E outcomes, correction loop count, input and output tokens, and recorded LLM cost.

Before calculating metrics, the records are checked for duplicate issue entries, valid issue-to-branch associations, and consistency between task-specific JSON test cases and the final E2E report. Where a task has multiple related branches or changes, only attributable final production-code changes are used. The extraction cutoff for confirmed Jira-linked defects is recorded with the dataset and applied consistently across workflow conditions. The status-history timestamps used for lead time follow the *In Progress* to *Development Complete* definition in Section 4.2.1.

Missing records are distinguished from observed values of zero. For example, an unavailable E2E report is not treated as a 0% pass rate, and absent LLM usage data is not treated as zero tokens or zero cost. Each outcome is calculated only when its required inputs are available and valid. A zero denominator, such as no attributable changed production-code lines or no recorded tokens, makes the corresponding ratio undefined; the issue can still contribute to other valid outcomes. The number of usable tasks and the reasons for missing or excluded measurements are reported separately for each metric and workflow condition.

Valid extreme observations, including high defect densities for small changes, are retained. The associated raw defect counts and changed LOC are preserved to support interpretation rather than removing such cases solely because their ratios are unusually large.

**Descriptive Analysis**

The distribution of tasks is summarized by workflow condition, developer, and the simple, medium, and complex task categories defined in Section 4.1.2. For each principal metric, the analysis reports the available sample size, mean, median, standard deviation, and range. Median and interquartile range are also used to describe skewed measures such as lead time, defect density, and token cost.

Task-level E2E pass rates are calculated against each task's applicable JSON test cases. Because tasks may have different numbers of test cases, the analysis reports task-level distributions rather than assuming that every feature has the same test coverage. Defect counts and changed LOC are retained alongside task-level defect density. Where useful, a workflow group's pooled defect density is calculated as its total confirmed defects divided by its total changed production-code lines, expressed per thousand lines; this supplements rather than replaces the task-level analysis.

**Comparative Analysis**

The primary comparison examines the observed differences between the baseline and Trade-Harness for delivery lead time (RQ1), final E2E pass rate and defect density (RQ2), and changed LOC per million tokens and LLM token cost per resolved issue (RQ3). Differences are presented in their original units and, when the baseline value is nonzero and a relative comparison is meaningful, as percentage differences. Lower lead time, defect density, and token cost are interpreted as favourable in isolation; higher E2E pass rate is favourable. Changed LOC per million tokens is interpreted more cautiously as change volume relative to model usage, not as a direct measure of functional value or code quality.

Results are presented first for the overall workflow groups and then stratified by task complexity and by developer. The group sizes and task distributions are shown alongside the metric comparisons so that an overall difference is not attributed to the workflow without considering differences in assigned work. Where subgroup counts are small, the results are described without making strong generalizations.

Correction loop count is analysed as a supplementary process measure, alongside the final E2E outcome. The analysis examines the distribution of recorded test-script correction loops and identifies any instances that exceed the shared three-loop instruction. A difference in loop count is not treated as direct evidence of better software quality or a specific context or memory-management failure.

**Interpretation of Results**

The study emphasizes descriptive comparisons, the magnitude and consistency of differences, and their practical implications across task-complexity levels. No statistical significance test is prescribed before the observed sample sizes, distributions, and repeated observations within developers have been assessed. Since tasks were not randomly assigned and each of the two developers contributes multiple observations, task records are not assumed to be independent experimental replications.

The analysis considers delivery efficiency, software quality, and computational cost together, including possible trade-offs between them. Observed differences are interpreted as associations between the workflow conditions and engineering outcomes in this case setting. They do not establish that Trade-Harness alone caused an improvement or that a particular layer, such as context or memory management, explains the result. These limitations are considered further in Section 4.3.
