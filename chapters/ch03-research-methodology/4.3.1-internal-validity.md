#### 4.3.1 Internal Validity

Tasks are assigned by the technical lead rather than randomly allocated. The lead must consider business domain, estimated effort, and developer availability, so the two workflow conditions may receive tasks with different requirements even within the same complexity category. The study aims to balance assignments across workflows for each developer and complexity level, using estimates made before implementation. This reduces obvious allocation differences but does not make the tasks equivalent. The feature-development scope is applied consistently across conditions, and the final sample and any exclusions must be reported to avoid selecting tasks based on observed outcomes.

The two participating developers also differ in experience and familiarity with individual parts of the system. Both use the baseline and Trade-Harness to avoid confounding a workflow with one developer, and results are examined separately by developer where the sample permits. Nevertheless, familiarity with a business module, existing code, or dependencies may favour a particular task. Differences in business priorities, interruptions, and team workload during the 12-week study can also affect delivery time without being caused by the workflow.

Learning and carryover effects are possible because the developers work in the same environment and use both conditions over time. Experience with Trade-Harness may influence later prompting practices in the baseline, while repeated use of either workflow may make developers more proficient. The same task is not implemented twice, which avoids the direct advantage of repeating a solution, but does not eliminate learning across different tasks.

The developers know which workflow they are using, and expectations about the proposed orchestration approach may influence the amount of guidance, review, or manual intervention provided. The comparison uses shared project requirements and existing Jira, GitLab, E2E, and LLM usage records rather than relying solely on subjective ratings. These records make the observations traceable, although they cannot remove differences in human decisions. Both conditions receive the same instruction to limit E2E test-script correction to three loops, but Trade-Harness enforces the limit through workflow controls whereas the baseline relies on instructions. Any observed deviation is reported as a difference in protocol compliance; it may affect testing effort and cost, and it cannot automatically be attributed to context loss or memory failure.
