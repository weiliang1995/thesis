#### 2.4.3 Research Gap and Evaluation Rationale

The reviewed studies investigate context policies, handoff information, agent architecture, planning and resource limits under different evaluation conditions. They provide empirical evidence that these decisions can affect expenditure and task resolution, with effects depending on the model, resource policy and task setting. However, the studies mapped in this section do not directly establish how two task-level workflows using the same coding-agent tool compare in resource use when applied to enterprise front-end development spanning multiple repositories. In particular, autonomous benchmark attempts differ from development tasks that include developer decisions, implementation, testing and handoff under organisational requirements. The task categories used in this study also represent estimated development effort rather than model-specific difficulty or a prescribed number of inference turns.

The evaluation considers task-associated input and output tokens as a measure of recorded model use. The gateway's recorded LLM charge per issue provides a distinct monetary observation, since identical token counts need not incur identical charges. Neither is a measure of total computational work, developer labour or overall project cost. Changed production lines of code per million tokens relates the volume of a final code change to the corresponding recorded model use. As a descriptive ratio, it helps contextualise consumption when tasks involve different amounts of modification, but a larger ratio does not necessarily indicate a more useful or higher-quality implementation. Concise repairs and deletions can have substantial functional value, and changes of similar size can require different amounts of investigation. Therefore this measure should be interpreted alongside task characteristics, delivery lead time and quality rather than treated as a standalone productivity score.

The analysis compares these observations across the conventional prompt-based and structured agent orchestration workflows, considering the task-complexity categories defined in Chapter 3. A workflow may consume additional tokens while producing favourable delivery or quality observations, or consume fewer tokens without such improvements. The literature does not prescribe a direction for the comparison. Furthermore, task-associated gateway records do not reliably distinguish tokens spent on planning, memory, repository exploration, implementation and validation. The quasi-experimental comparison therefore cannot isolate the contribution of any particular mechanism, establish how much consumption was redundant, or determine an optimal adaptive policy. It addresses RQ3 at the level of the observed workflow and its resource-use measures.
