#### 2.2.3 Research Gap and Evaluation Rationale

The reviewed studies leave a narrower question than whether AI assistance makes developers faster in general. Experiments with and without coding assistance measure the effect of access to particular tools (Peng et al., 2023; Paradis et al., 2025; Becker et al., 2025). Comparisons of agent arrangements often report issue-resolution rates or model cost on benchmarks (Xia et al., 2024; Fan et al., 2026). Workplace studies document developer coordination and correction, while enterprise telemetry can show changes in pull-request output (Chou et al., 2026; Tang et al., 2026; Murphy-Hill et al., 2026). These findings do not directly answer how task completion time differs when the same agent-capable tool is used under developer-coordinated and structured orchestration workflows in multi-repository front-end development.

This study addresses that question through development lead time. For each eligible Jira task, the measure runs from its transition into *In Progress* to its transition into *Development Complete*. It covers elapsed time in the developer-owned stage, including time spent investigating, implementing, checking, and correcting a task, as well as any waiting that occurs before handover. It excludes later QA and deployment activities. Forsgren et al. (2021) describe the flow of work items and the delays they encounter as meaningful aspects of developer productivity, while cautioning against reducing productivity to a single measure. Development lead time is therefore suitable for evaluating the delivery-efficiency aspect of this comparison; the quality and computational-cost outcomes are examined in Sections 2.3 and 2.4.

A difference in lead time would indicate a difference between the observed workflow groups in this setting. On its own, it would not show that a particular instruction, memory record, validation step, or execution rule produced the difference. Task complexity, developer familiarity, dependencies, and waiting within the development stage also affect elapsed time. The comparison must consider these factors and allow for either workflow to have a shorter lead time. Accordingly, RQ1 asks how delivery lead time differs between the structured agent orchestration workflow and the conventional prompt-based workflow, without assuming the direction of that difference.
