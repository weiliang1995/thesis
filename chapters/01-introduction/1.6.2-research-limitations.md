#### 1.6.2 Research Limitations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the findings of this study. First, the study is conducted within an active enterprise development environment rather than a controlled laboratory setting. As a result, identical development tasks cannot be repeated under both workflow conditions, since commercial software tasks are completed only once as part of normal delivery activities. To reduce this limitation, tasks are categorized according to factors such as scope, story points, and cross-repository complexity, allowing comparisons to be made between tasks of similar characteristics.

Second, the non-deterministic nature of large language models may introduce variation in agent behaviour and generated outputs, even when similar instructions and workflows are used. This variability cannot be fully eliminated and may affect the consistency of the observed results.

Finally, factors such as task complexity, repository dependencies, existing code quality, and developer familiarity with the codebase may influence development outcomes. Although efforts are made to distribute tasks across comparable categories and involve multiple developers, these contextual factors cannot be completely controlled in a real-world software engineering environment. Therefore, the findings should be interpreted within the context of the study setting.
