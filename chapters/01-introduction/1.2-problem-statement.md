### 1.2 Problem Statement

AI coding agents can help with planning, implementation, testing, and code review, but delivering a feature still requires developers to interpret requirements, understand dependencies, and verify the final change. In a conventional developer-managed prompt-based workflow, developers decide how to divide work, what project context to provide, and how to coordinate the AI tool's outputs with the rest of the development process. These responsibilities are particularly relevant in enterprise front-end systems, where changes may span application modules, shared components, and multiple repositories. They raise three related concerns: delivery efficiency, software quality, and computational cost.

Structured agent orchestration offers a way to coordinate task execution, context handling, and validation across development stages. It may reduce repeated manual coordination or missed dependencies, but its additional planning and validation activities can also introduce overhead. The studies reviewed in Chapters 2 and 3 examine aspects of AI-assisted coding, context management, agent-based issue resolution, and resource efficiency. However, they provide limited direct evidence comparing a complete structured orchestration workflow with developer-managed prompt-based AI assistance on real enterprise front-end tasks across delivery, quality, and cost outcomes together. As a result, the practical effectiveness and trade-offs of structured orchestration in this setting remain uncertain.

To address this gap, this study compares Trade-Harness with a conventional prompt-based AI-assisted workflow in a multi-repository front-end development environment. It evaluates delivery lead time, software quality, and computational resource efficiency using data from actual development tasks. The three concerns are discussed in Sections 1.2.1–1.2.3, while the corresponding objectives and research questions are defined in Sections 1.3 and 1.4.
