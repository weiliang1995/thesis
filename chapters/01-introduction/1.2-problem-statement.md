### 1.2 Problem Statement

AI-assisted development can help with implementation, but delivering a feature involves more than producing code. Developers must interpret requirements, identify dependencies, check changes, and address problems found during testing and review. In a conventional prompt-based workflow, developers are responsible for coordinating how the AI tool is used across these activities, even when the tool itself can perform agentic actions. In an enterprise front-end system, this work may involve multiple repositories, shared components, and dependencies that are not visible from a single file.

Structured agent orchestration offers a different approach by defining how tasks, context, execution stages, and validation are managed. However, additional planning, agent actions, and validation steps may also increase the time and tokens needed to finish a task. It is therefore unclear how a structured workflow compares with developer-managed prompting when both are used for real development work.

This study examines that problem through three related concerns: workflow coordination and delivery efficiency, context continuity and software quality, and computational resource efficiency. The comparison evaluates the outcomes of the complete workflow, rather than attempting to attribute each outcome to an individual Trade-Harness layer.
