#### 1.1.1 Agentic AI in Software Engineering

Nowadays, the emergence of agentic capabilities reframes the role of AI in software engineering from assistance to autonomy (Otoum & Elkhalili, 2026). In Li et al. (2025) findings, agentic AI has unique strengths in software engineering tasks such as features development, bug fixing and software testing.

The previous AI coding assistants were limited to code completion, suggestions and implementation, leaving the developer to control input prompt, context identification, and task execution. Autonomous coding agents, on the other hand, are capable of planning and executing multi-step development tasks, interacting with the repository and development environment, assessing partial results, and adjusting their implementations according to the feedback received from the validation process.

Although greater agent autonomy can reduce human effort, it does not necessarily make the results reliable because the quality of the output depends heavily on the context retrieved during task execution. This is a major challenge in industrial settings, where codebases are often large and spread across different repositories. Therefore, developers may need a structured workflow to standardize how AI agents retrieve context appropriately during task execution. However, there is now lacking of empirical studies showing that agentic workflows help to increase delivery efficiency, improve code quality and reduce token costs.
