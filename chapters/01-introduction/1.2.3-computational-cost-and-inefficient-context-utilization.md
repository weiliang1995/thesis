#### 1.2.3 Computational Cost and Inefficient Context Utilization

Yao et al. (2022) showed that complex tasks often require more information to guide the model’s next steps. In software development tasks, this means that the same files, type definitions, dependency information, or previous changes may be loaded repeatedly across different stages of the workflow. This increases prompt size, token usage and runtime, but it does not necessarily improve the quality of the generated output.

Without a structured workflow to optimize AI agent to reuse the relevant context, it will end up repeatedly inject the same context in a multi-step workflow or iterative tasks, such as feature development or multiple rounds of code review. Furthermore, agents may get stuck in repetitive reasoning loops, constantly generating similar intermediate steps without updating their plans, wasting tokens and yielding no real progress (Yao et al., 2022).

In large-scale front-end development, without a standardized workflow to manage the context, it will not only lead to inconsistent result quality and require frequent back-and-forth modifications and adjustments, but also increase the overall process time and waste unnecessary tokens.
