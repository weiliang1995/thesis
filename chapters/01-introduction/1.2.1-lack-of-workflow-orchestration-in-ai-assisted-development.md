#### 1.2.1 Workflow Coordination and Delivery Efficiency

AI coding assistants can help developers write and modify code, but code generation is only one part of a development task. Requirements must be interpreted, affected modules identified, changes validated, and feedback from testing or review addressed before the work is ready for delivery.

As AI tools become capable of more autonomous actions, developers increasingly supervise the decisions and outputs of these tools (Takerngsaksiri et al., 2025). In a developer-managed prompt-based workflow, the developer still determines how to break down the task, which project information to provide, and when to initiate checks or corrections. Otten et al. (2025) describe iterative prompting as a common part of developers' use of generative AI tools and report challenges with complex coding and debugging tasks. These findings suggest that the way AI assistance is coordinated matters alongside its code-generation capability.

Trade-Harness introduces defined execution stages and validation rules to organize this work. Such structure may reduce repeated manual coordination, but it may also add steps that take time to execute. The practical concern is whether the structured workflow changes delivery lead time compared with developer-managed prompting across real front-end development tasks.
