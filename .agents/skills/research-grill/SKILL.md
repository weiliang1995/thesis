# Research Grill

Facilitate a sustained, detail-level research-design interrogation. The goal is to expose unresolved design choices, hidden assumptions and missing operational detail before the researcher treats a study decision as settled. The researcher owns decisions; the agent investigates facts and persistently challenges reasoning. Do not rewrite the manuscript during the interview.

## Process

1. **Ground the decision.** Read the [project map](../../../AGENTS.md), [thesis standards](../../docs/thesis.md), [glossary](../../docs/glossary.md), [research mapping](../../docs/research-mapping.md) and relevant [decision records](../../docs/decisions/README.md). Use the [thesis structure](../../docs/architecture.md) to find the relevant active sections. If an expected file or directory is absent, record that and use available sources. Establish the question, current proposal, constraints, confirmed decisions and snapshot. Distinguish the author's proposal from established evidence.
2. **Build a decision and detail map.** Identify the decision's dependencies and consequences for research questions, constructs, population, sampling, treatment and comparator, procedures, measures, data availability, analysis, validity and interpretation. For each applicable area, distinguish (a) specified and supported, (b) specified but unverified, (c) underspecified, (d) unresolved researcher choice, and (e) not applicable with rationale. Do not let a high-level choice conceal missing operational details. Separate answerable factual questions from researcher choices. Investigate answerable facts using original sources or available research records. Report inaccessible evidence rather than guessing.
3. **Interrogate in adaptive rounds.** Ask a small batch of specific, answerable questions, then wait for the researcher's reply. For each question explain why the detail matters and what depends on it. Probe vague terms, unexamined assumptions, feasibility, edge cases, measurement boundaries and the consequences of alternatives. When an answer exposes a gap or contradiction, follow that thread with the next focused question before moving on. Do not accept "TBD", "standard practice", an assertion of feasibility, or a preferred outcome as resolved without asking what evidence or operational rule supports it. Do not repeat settled questions. Maintain a concise ledger of resolved details, open questions, dependencies and evidence to verify, and update it after each reply. Keep grilling relevant to the decision and its consequences rather than expanding into unrelated critique.
4. **Test the design under concrete cases.** Use examples, hypothetical observations or edge cases to check whether another researcher could apply the procedure and whether the measure or analysis would yield an unambiguous result. Mark hypothetical examples as illustrations, never as study data. Check disconfirming explanations, rival interpretations, null outcomes and the option of retaining the existing design. Recommend an option only where evidence and constraints justify it. Do not retrofit a method to achieve a desirable outcome.
5. **Resolve terminology.** When a definition or scope distinction is implicated, use research-terminology. Keep unresolved terms provisional.
6. **Close only when ready.** Do not move to confirmation merely because one plausible answer has emerged. First review the ledger with the researcher and ask about material gaps, contradictions, unverified assumptions and unresolved dependencies. The researcher may explicitly pause or narrow the grilling. Otherwise continue focused rounds until every material design choice is resolved, explicitly deferred with a dependency, or justified as out of scope. A deferred or unverified detail remains open and must not be presented as a complete design.
7. **Confirm.** Summarize the proposed decision, rationale, alternatives considered, resolved specifications, open details, evidence still needed, uncertainty, limitations, affected sections and required follow-up. Ask the researcher to confirm, revise or leave it open. Treat silence or conversational momentum as no approval. If blocked by missing data or supervisor input, identify that dependency.
8. **Record.** For a consequential decision, use [the RDR format](references/rdr-format.md) after explicit confirmation. Ask before recording unresolved proposals as durable decisions. Propose manuscript or mapping changes separately; apply them only when authorized.

## Completion

The session leaves an updated detail ledger and a clear state for each material branch. A decision is ready to confirm only when its consequential operational details have been surfaced, dependencies and evidence limits are explicit, and the researcher confirms it. Deferred, unverified and incomplete details remain visible as open. Researcher confirmation does not imply supervisor approval or empirical validation.
