# Research Terminology

Maintain a precise shared language for the thesis. This is definition work, not a passive consistency review. For a read-only audit, use thesis-review's terminology axis when the researcher invokes it.

## Process

1. Read AGENTS.md, GLOSSARY.md, relevant manuscript definitions and usages, cited original literature and relevant confirmed research decisions. Identify what is already agreed, provisional or contradictory.
2. Challenge the term: what does it refer to, what does it exclude, where does it overlap with other concepts, and what would be a concrete counterexample? Distinguish a literature-based conceptual definition from this study's operational definition. Identify alternatives and evidence, including variation in definitions across sources. Do not invent artificial boundaries.
3. For a measured construct, check the metric, numerator/denominator, unit, scope, observation window, start/end events and aggregation where applicable. Mark unspecified fields as pending rather than inferring them. Distinguish canonical naming from quoted source vocabulary.
4. Present a proposed glossary entry using [the glossary format](references/glossary-format.md), with precise source locations, alternative terms, limitations and affected manuscript sections. If a term change would alter the study design or interpretation, recommend a research-grill discussion before confirmation.
5. Obtain explicit researcher confirmation before adding or changing approved glossary entries. Update GLOSSARY.md only for confirmed definitions; preserve uncertainty and link superseded decisions where applicable. Do not treat historical drafts as approved definitions. Propose cross-chapter manuscript changes separately and apply only when authorized.

## Completion

A confirmed term has a traceable definition and applicable operational details, or explicit pending fields. Conflicts and affected sections are surfaced. No manuscript edits or new research decisions are made implicitly.
