# Author Writing Profile

Version: 0.1.1

## Basis and scope

Qualitative observations from the imported Proposal and selected Chapter 1 review commits, approved as a preliminary interpretation by the author on 2026-11-27. These are mixed-provenance, author-reviewed samples; Git authorship alone does not establish who composed the words. Three additional author-reviewed AI exercises dated 2026-11-27 are indexed in [revision-samples.md](revision-samples.md). They measure editing preferences rather than independent writing style: the supplied text may anchor the author's choices. No corpus statistics, independent writing experiment, or detector validation were performed. Preferences below are descriptive, not mechanical rules.

| Dimension | Observed tendency | Editorial implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vocabulary | Ordinary English with domain-specific terms; formal academic vocabulary accepted when appropriate | Prefer precise language; do not simplify just because a word is formal |
| Sentence structure | Direct syntax, including long sentences with accumulated technical detail | Retain understandable constructions; split or combine only when clarity improves |
| Grammar | Historical drafts contain occasional agreement, article and verb-form errors | Correct errors that affect correctness; do not reproduce errors as style |
| Transitions | Familiar connections such as However, Although, Therefore, For example | Keep when they convey a real relationship; no banned-word list |
| Explanation | Direct definitions and functions; concrete engineering examples when relevant | Keep relevant examples; request missing examples from the author |
| Terminology | Repeated use of established technical labels | Preserve terms rather than varying them for decoration |
| Tone | Formal and direct; engineer explaining a technical topic | Avoid unnecessary sophistication; retain meaningful qualifications |
| Scope | Redundant modifiers are sometimes removed during review | Remove only when redundant; research-scope changes require approval |
| Paragraph structure | Related information was combined in one exercise | Allow natural grouping without removing substantive information |
| Revision behaviour | Clear existing passages were sometimes retained almost verbatim | No change is a valid editorial outcome; preserve provenance |

## Calibration observations by passage type

| Exercise | Observed edits | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Context engineering | Reorganised definition and examples; simplified abstract phrasing | Direct definition and compact explanation are useful candidates, not universal rules |
| RAG | Revised definition; retained most mechanism and limitation wording | Formal language may be acceptable when the mechanism is clearly described |
| ReAct literature review | Abbreviated terms; retained most prose | Acceptance of conventional academic structure, not evidence of independent composition |

These are three task-specific observations, not quantitative or generalisable results. Distinguish active revisions from unchanged AI prose. Check for semantic drift before treating any change as an editorial preference.

## Academic boundaries

Research accuracy, claim strength, citations, defined terms, and original reasoning take priority over style. A historical example may use outdated methodology, terminology or assumptions. Do not carry those facts forward. Do not infer a preference from a single edit or treat a substantive research correction as a stylistic rule.

## Updating the profile

Record author-approved corrections with the original and revised excerpt, commit or source reference, and a brief explanation. Distinguish verified author-written material from AI-assisted or uncertain material. Change this profile only on explicit request. Use feedback from ordinary review work rather than requiring additional exercises.
