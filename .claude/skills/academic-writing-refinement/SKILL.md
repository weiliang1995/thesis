# Academic Writing Refinement

Version: 0.1.1 (qualitative editing preferences, calibrated)

## Purpose

Support author-led revision of thesis prose. Maintain the author's natural academic English without sacrificing meaning, evidence, or readability. Do not equate polished English with research quality.

## References

- Read [author-profile.md](references/author-profile.md) for observed preferences and their limitations.
- Read [revision-samples.md](references/revision-samples.md) when comparing phrasing or reviewing a substantial passage. This contains provenance-linked historical examples and an index of three calibration exercises, not templates to copy. Read individual calibration files only when needed to understand the author's feedback.
- Read the repository's AGENTS.md and any applicable local instructions before editing.

## Workflow

1. Read the requested section and its immediate context. Identify the author's intended argument, terminology, citations, and any established research definitions.
2. Review for unclear sentences, unnecessary wording, inaccurate terminology, unsupported claims, and excessive polishing. Distinguish language issues from substantive research issues.
3. Default to review-only: provide the original excerpt, suggested local change, and a brief reason. If the author explicitly requests editing, make the smallest necessary changes; do not rewrite unaffected paragraphs. A no-change result is valid if the text is already clear, accurate, and acceptable under institutional AI-use rules. Do not assume that a passage needs revision simply because AI generated it.
4. Ask the author to supply missing reasoning, evidence, or examples; do not invent author opinions, experience, data, dates, citations, or results. Historical examples are language references only, not current research facts.
5. Check the revised text against the original. Preserve meaning, citation association, technical definitions, numerical values, uncertainty, and chapter-level alignment. Report unresolved issues separately.
6. Show a concise change summary or diff. Do not commit or publish thesis edits unless the author requests it.

## Language principles

- Clarity and precision take precedence over elegance.
- Prefer precise, familiar words when they convey the same meaning; retain appropriate formal academic vocabulary and technical terms. Do not force synonyms for defined concepts.
- Allow direct sentences, useful detail, and familiar transitions. Organise sentences and paragraphs according to meaning, not fixed length or rhythm. Correct grammatical errors without over-polishing.
- Do not add deliberate grammatical errors or artificial variation.
- Do not use detector scores as an editing target or claim that the resulting prose is human-written. Preserve the history of AI assistance and follow institutional disclosure requirements.

## Review by passage type

- Concept introduction: check that the definition and function are accurate and understandable; combine related information only if no substantive detail is lost.
- Technical mechanism: check sequence, dependencies, and technical precision; leave a clear existing explanation intact.
- Literature review: distinguish source findings, interpretation, and limitations. Preserve attribution and appropriate qualification; flag claims that need source verification.
- Across all passages, distinguish active author edits from acceptance of existing AI prose. Neither acceptance nor stylistic consistency establishes independent authorship. Avoid unsupported generalisations from the small sample.

## Output

For review-only: findings, local suggestions, and questions requiring author judgment. For explicitly requested edits: revised passage, concise change summary, and any issues requiring verification. No unrelated rewrites.

## Evaluation

Use author acceptance, readability, factual and citation preservation, and consistency with approved thesis definitions as evaluation criteria. Treat the profile as provisional. Refine it using author-approved feedback from actual use; do not require additional calibration exercises. Record both accepted and rejected suggestions, with provenance. Do not infer detector performance from style or acceptance.
