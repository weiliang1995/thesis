## 4.7 Summary of Findings

This chapter presented a descriptive comparison of 548 Jira issues across two AI-assisted front-end development workflows. Overall results give context, while task-complexity comparisons are the main analysis and developer-level comparisons are supplementary.

**Table 4.15. Provisional observations for the three research questions**

| Research question | Overall comparison (Baseline / Trade-Harness) | Complexity-level qualification |
|---|---|---|
| RQ1: Delivery efficiency | Mean lead time 1.719 / 1.717 days; median 0.88 / 0.88 days | Almost identical for simple tasks; mean lead time modestly lower under Trade-Harness in medium and complex groups |
| RQ2: Software quality | Mean final E2E pass rate 91.55% / 93.35%; mean task-level defect density 3.06 / 2.41 per KLOC | E2E mean higher under Trade-Harness in all three groups; mean defect density higher under Trade-Harness in medium and complex groups |
| RQ3: Resource efficiency and cost | Mean changed LOC / million tokens 18.09 / 15.87; mean LLM cost $50.89 / $62.19 per task | Trade-Harness has higher mean cost in each complexity group; changed LOC per token is not a direct measure of productivity |
| Supplementary process measure | Mean correction loops 1.00 / 0.75; tasks above instructed three-loop maximum: 8 / 0 | Process observations only; not direct evidence of code quality |

The current dataset shows no clear overall lead-time separation. Trade-Harness is associated with a higher mean final E2E pass rate and lower overall mean task-level defect density, accompanied by greater LLM cost per task. The direction and size of differences are not fully consistent across task complexity and developer.

These results are descriptive and do not establish causation. The discussion chapter will consider the practical trade-offs and limitations of the nonrandomized, production-based comparison.
